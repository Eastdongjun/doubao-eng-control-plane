# Cognition, Emotion, and Ethics

Use this module when the experience is complex, stressful, trust-sensitive, persuasive, emotionally charged, or intended for repeated expert use.

## Cognitive design

Reduce avoidable load by:

- Keeping essential context and state visible
- Chunking by meaning and task
- Presenting choices only when relevant
- Using stable location, language, and mapping
- Providing examples, previews, comparison, history, and sensible defaults
- Converting recall into recognition
- Avoiding simultaneous decisions that can be sequenced
- Making system rules explainable at the point of consequence

Do not equate fewer visible elements with lower cognitive load. Hidden state, ambiguous icons, mystery navigation, and inconsistent behavior increase mental work.

## Decision architecture

For each decision specify:

- The question the user is answering
- Information needed to answer it
- Options and meaningful differences
- Default and why it is safe
- Consequence, scope, timing, and reversibility
- Confidence or uncertainty
- What happens if the user postpones

Use defaults to reduce work only when they are safe, expected, reversible, and disclosed. Do not exploit inertia.

Useful mechanisms include:

- Orientation for uncertainty
- Preview for commitment anxiety
- Calm, precise language for risk
- Visible progress for effort
- Preservation of work for trust
- Specific recovery for failure
- Proportional acknowledgment for achievement

Do not use delight to distract from unclear pricing, permissions, data use, irreversible consequences, or poor reliability.

## Trust model

Trust grows when the system is:

- Competent: works and explains limits
- Predictable: similar actions produce similar results
- Legible: status, source, scope, and consequence are visible
- Controllable: users can pause, cancel, edit, undo, or appeal
- Honest: uncertainty, advertising, automation, and data use are disclosed
- Recoverable: failure does not erase effort or trap the user

Design confidence calibration. Do not make uncertain automated output look certain.

## Ethical boundaries

Reject:

- Forced continuity, hidden cancellation, confirmshaming, disguised advertising
- False scarcity, fabricated social proof, preselected consent, privacy obstruction
- Roach motels, bait-and-switch, obstruction, nagging, misdirection
- Variable reward mechanics that exploit vulnerability without user benefit
- Artificial urgency or emotional pressure in high-stakes decisions

For persuasive goals, document:

- Intended user benefit
- Business incentive
- Conflict between them
- Vulnerable populations
- User control and exit
- Guardrail metric and review owner

## Expertise and frequency

- Novices need orientation, safe defaults, examples, and learnable structure.
- Intermediates need continuity, visible state, and progressive power.
- Experts need speed, density, shortcuts, batching, customization, and reliable undo.

Do not force experts through repeated education or force novices to memorize expert syntax. Build one coherent model with progressive capability.
