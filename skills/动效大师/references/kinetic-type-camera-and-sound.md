## Kinetic typography

Treat type as speech and composition in time:

- Reading order and minimum exposure
- Phrase, word, syllable, or glyph as the unit
- Entry, articulation, emphasis, hold, and exit
- Weight, width, slant, tracking, baseline, mask, and variable-font axes
- Relationship to voice, music, image, and camera
- Language, CJK, RTL, diacritics, line wrapping, and font loading

Choose behavior by meaning:

- Reveal for discovery
- Compression for urgency
- Expansion for release
- Repetition for insistence
- Interruption for conflict
- Alignment for resolution
- Variable-axis change for transformation

Do not sacrifice reading to demonstrate typography.

## Camera role

Choose one primary role:

- **Observer:** stable, objective, lets subjects move
- **Guide:** moves attention through information or space
- **Participant:** responds to action and feels embodied
- **Witness:** restrained movement conveys gravity or intimacy
- **Performer:** camera motion itself creates expression

Specify position, lens/perspective, path, acceleration, target, depth, occlusion, and cut logic. Avoid perpetual drifting camera movement.

## Editing grammar

Use:

- Match cut for conceptual or geometric continuity
- Hard cut for rupture, speed, contrast, or compression
- Dissolve for time, memory, or ambiguity
- Wipe/mask for graphic authorship or spatial reveal
- L-cut/J-cut for sound-led continuity
- Montage for accumulation
- Hold for consequence and breathing room

Transitions do not rescue weak shot or scene structure.

## Storyboard and animatic

For each beat define:

- Time range
- Frame/composition
- Action and transformation
- Camera
- Type/copy
- Sound/haptic
- Purpose and emotional shift
- Asset/technical note

Approve sequence and timing with an animatic before high-cost rendering.

## Sound

Use sound to:

- Confirm action
- Communicate material or spatial event
- Establish rhythm and tension
- Mark hierarchy or completion
- Preserve continuity across cuts

Specify:

- Optional versus required
- User control, autoplay policy, and environment
- Repetition and fatigue
- Loudness, spectrum, masking, and device variation
- Latency relationship to visual event
- Captions, transcript, and non-audio alternative
- Asset rights and version

Silence must remain a complete experience.

## Haptics

Use haptics for:

- Discrete confirmation
- Threshold crossing
- Constraint or boundary
- Error or warning
- Material/impact cue

Match strength and pattern to significance. Do not vibrate continuously for decorative texture. Provide a visual alternative and respect platform/user settings.

## Multisensory synchronization

Decide whether channels are:

- Synchronous for impact
- Offset for anticipation or echo
- Complementary for richer information
- Redundant for accessibility

Test on real hardware. Perceived synchronization varies with audio path, rendering latency, display refresh, and haptic hardware.
