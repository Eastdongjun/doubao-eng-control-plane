## Research depth

Choose proportionally:

- **Calibration:** routine product flow; 3–6 references focused on behavior and restraint.
- **Focused field:** signature feature or brand moment; 10–20 diverse references across several media.
- **Deep field:** identity, campaign, title, cultural/historical, or unfamiliar language; research lineage, primary artifacts, current expression, technical mechanism, and misuse risk until the grammar stabilizes.

These are starting ranges, not quotas. Stop when new evidence no longer changes the direction space, risks, or grammar.

## Taste discovery

Collect concrete evidence:

- Current brand/product motion
- Two to five admired references
- Two to five anti-references
- Non-screen references: material, architecture, dance, fashion, music, film, machinery, weather, craft
- Forced choices: calm vs energetic, crisp vs organic, direct vs theatrical, flat vs spatial, timeless vs current
- Tolerance for surprise, interruption, abstraction, sound, and novelty
- Decision-maker disagreements and authority

For each reference ask:

- What exact moment creates the reaction?
- Is the reaction caused by rhythm, scale, material, camera, type, edit, sound, or context?
- What would become annoying on the tenth repetition?
- What quality should be translated, and what surface treatment should not?

Separate personal preference, brand aspiration, audience expectation, and category norm.

## Reference field

Research across independent lenses:

1. Product interaction and platform motion
2. Graphic design and kinetic typography
3. Film titles, editing, cinematography, and VFX
4. Animation principles and character/object performance
5. Physical materials, mechanics, light, and optics
6. Dance, choreography, music, and rhythm
7. Generative, spatial, game, and interactive art
8. Historical/cultural source material when named
9. Current production and trend signals
10. Anti-references and failed excess

A social feed is a discovery surface, not evidence of universality.

## Source ledger

For every decision-relevant source record:

| Source | Date | Type | Direct observation | Inference | Transferable mechanism | Do not copy | Rights/status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Label:

- `Source fact`
- `Direct observation`
- `Inference`
- `Proposed translation`

Do not present memory or visual impression as researched history.

## Trend analysis

For any named trend, build a trend passport:

- Name and competing names
- Lineage and earlier analogues
- Current drivers: platform, tooling, culture, technology, fashion
- Recognizable mechanisms
- What is genuinely new versus revived
- Functional strengths
- Accessibility/performance/cultural risks
- Category saturation
- Shelf-life and expiry signals
- Translation into a system
- What remains deliberately untrendy

Examples such as liquid glass are material hypotheses, not one-click styles. Inspect transparency, refraction, depth, background dependency, legibility, motion parallax, input response, performance, and platform context before use.

## Direction comparison

Create alternatives only where comparison can change a costly decision. Hold content, duration, viewport, and assets constant.

Each direction must differ by mechanism, not merely color:

- Kinetic personality
- Temporal structure
- Material and spatial model
- Type/camera/edit relationship
- Signature behavior
- System scalability
- Accessibility and performance risk

Choose using the aesthetic jury in `SKILL.md`. Record why the rejected directions lost and what would justify revisiting them.

## Cultural and authorship boundaries

- Verify period, geography, medium, restoration, and contemporary reinterpretation.
- Distinguish source artifact from later revival and commercial cliché.
- Translate compositional or temporal principles; do not paste motifs onto unrelated behavior.
- Do not imitate a living creator’s recognizable signature or copy a distinctive sequence.
- Verify rights for fonts, footage, music, sound, logos, and source imagery.
- Attribute research without implying endorsement.
